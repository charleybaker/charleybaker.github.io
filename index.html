About

Born and raised in Vancouver, BC, I am an economics and development professional with a strong foundation in quantitative analysis, research and financial markets. I completed a Bachelor of Arts in Joint Honours Economics and Global Development at Queen's University, where I was also a varsity women's volleyball athlete, before pursuing a Master of Specialised Economic Analysis in International Trade, Finance and Development at the Barcelona School of Economics, which I completed in July 2026. My professional experience spans workforce planning and business intelligence at Vancity, macroeconomic and development policy research at the John Deutsch Institute, and equity research in critical assets at Haywood Securities. Across these roles, I have developed strong analytical, technical and communication skills, with experience in SQL, Excel, Power BI, R, Python and Stata. I am eager to bring my curiosity, initiative and collaborative spirit to a team where I can continue learning and contribute meaningfully.

CourseWork

Climate Damage Estimation
This essay examines methodological disagreement in empirical climate-economy research, focusing on whether aggregate temperature-GDP relationships can provide credible estimates of climate damages. It evaluates competing empirical approaches, including country-level panel regressions, cross-sectional methods and structural models, with particular attention to identification, adaptation and external validity. The analysis considers how these methodological limitations affect the use of climate damage estimates in policy and argues for greater reliance on sectoral evidence, complementary identification strategies and transparent treatment of uncertainty.
