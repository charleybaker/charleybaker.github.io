Thesis

Structural Transformation in Africa: Disaggregating Services and Productivity Dynamics
This thesis examines the sectoral reallocation of labour across African economies, focusing on why agricultural workers are flowing into consumer services rather than manufacturing. Using a multi-sector growth model calibrated to thirteen African economies over 1990–2018, the research decomposes structural transformation into productivity and reallocation channels. The counterfactual analysis reveals substantial heterogeneity across countries in the role consumer service productivity plays in driving agricultural exit, establishing that the developmental significance of service sector expansion depends critically on underlying productivity dynamics rather than employment scale alone. The work contributes methodologically through the extension of Duarte and Restuccia's framework to isolate consumer services from the broader service aggregate, and provides empirical evidence relevant to understanding low-income country development trajectories.

Coursework

Climate Damage Estimation
This essay examines methodological disagreement in empirical climate-economy research, focusing on whether aggregate temperature-GDP relationships can provide credible estimates of climate damages. It evaluates competing empirical approaches, including country-level panel regressions, cross-sectional methods and structural models, with particular attention to identification, adaptation and external validity. The analysis considers how these methodological limitations affect the use of climate damage estimates in policy and argues for greater reliance on sectoral evidence, complementary identification strategies and transparent treatment of uncertainty.
Sierra Leone's Diamond Economy: Institutional Analysis
This country study traces how corruption perpetuates Sierra Leone's economic stagnation, particularly through the blood diamond trade and weak institutional frameworks. The analysis evaluates formal and informal institutional failures, examines historical pathways to corruption, and proposes targeted interventions addressing both institutional capacity and social norms to break entrenched cycles of corrupt practice.
Poverty Assessment in Malawi: Rural-Urban Divergence
This poverty assessment examines Malawi's paradox of sustained economic growth alongside persistent poverty using household survey data and multiple analytical approaches. By disaggregating poverty across geography, household structure, and time, the analysis reveals that structural barriers in rural areas and uneven human capital distribution drive poverty outcomes that aggregate statistics obscure.
Hurricane Maria: Policy & Resilience
This policy report examines the economic and institutional vulnerabilities exposed by Hurricane Maria in Puerto Rico, focusing on disaster insurance, federal relief and energy infrastructure. It evaluates policy failures and draws on international case studies and risk modelling frameworks to develop recommendations for improving economic resilience.
Geospatial Analysis of African Population and Infrastructure
This geospatial analysis integrates Natural Earth datasets to map population distributions and examine accessibility to air transportation infrastructure across African countries. Using spatial joins and distance calculations, the work demonstrates how to coordinate multiple data sources for continent-wide infrastructure analysis and identify geographic patterns in population-infrastructure relationships.
Production Deployment of Demand Forecasting for Glovo
This project documents the production deployment of a weekly demand forecasting model for courier operations in Barcelona, covering end-to-end architecture, data pipelines, and model evaluation. The work establishes CI/CD workflows, multi-environment isolation, and automated validation to transition from experimental notebooks to a reliable operational system that Operations depends on weekly.
Quantitative Analysis of the Distributional Effects of Preferential Trade Agreements
This analysis applies the Heckscher-Ohlin model to assess distributional consequences of preferential trade agreements for a small open economy, distinguishing effects across worker and capitalist populations. The work designs redistribution policies that secure trade approval whilst maximising worker welfare under varying factor ownership structures and economic specialisation scenarios.
Industrial Policy Under State Capacity Constraints - Policy Brief
This policy brief reassesses industrial policy for Bangladesh amid fragmented global trade, examining how traditional instruments interact with state capacity constraints and external integration. The analysis proposes shifting away from subsidy-heavy approaches toward coordination-based tools and public procurement mechanisms better suited to institutional limitations.
Remote Work and Mental Health: Geographic Heterogeneity
This paper extends aggregate findings on remote work to examine heterogeneous effects across UK regions and household types, revealing concentrated impacts among single mothers and non-metropolitan workers. Using doubly robust differences-in-differences estimation and occupational teleworkability as an exogenous source of variation, the analysis demonstrates that null aggregate effects mask meaningful variation in how flexible work shapes mental health outcomes.
